# Student podcast script guidelines, ages 7-15

These guidelines are for podcast scripts aimed at elementary and middle school listeners, roughly ages 7-15.

## Core writing skills

### Make abstract ideas concrete

Turn abstract ideas into everyday images. Use school, games, family routines, snacks, sports, weather, pets, rooms, tools, and familiar objects as bridges.

Example: instead of defining a cell nucleus in technical language, compare it to a school principal's office that sends instructions.

### Design characters and voice

Young listeners respond to personality. Give the host a clear voice: curious, playful, warm, and smart without sounding superior. Recurring helper roles can also work, such as a curious sidekick or an expert character, as long as they support the story.

### Pull attention back often

Use recurring attention hooks: questions, short quizzes, sound surprises, tiny challenges, or simple actions.

Examples:

- "What would you do?"
- "Hold that thought."
- "Tap your fingers twice if you caught the clue."
- "Listen for the sound that tells us the scene changed."

## Key script rules

### Start with story

The first 15 seconds should open with a story, strange fact, question, mystery, or immediate scene. Avoid slow introductions.

### Keep one core idea

Each episode should focus on one core idea. Do not overload the listener with side topics.

### Use simple sentences

Prefer short sentences. Avoid stacked clauses. Introduce at most one specialist term at a time, and explain it immediately in plain language.

### Write for SFX

Use sound to replace explanation when possible. Mark frequent SFX opportunities, such as footsteps, doors, clocks, buttons, crowds, paper rustles, music stings, comic slips, or magical transitions.

### Control length and rhythm

For elementary listeners, keep episodes within 10-15 minutes. Change a small unit or shift the audio mood every 3-5 minutes.

### Avoid lecturing

The tone should feel like an older sibling or guide who knows a lot and still likes to play. Keep eye-level curiosity. Use encouragement and invitation. Avoid scolding phrases such as "you should" or "you must" unless safety requires them.

## Completion checks

Before finishing, the writer must confirm:

- the script is a podcast script, not an article
- the hook starts within the first 15 seconds
- sentences are short enough to read aloud
- SFX, BGM, pause, tone, or pronunciation cues appear where useful
- the episode has one clear core idea
- the host voice is warm, curious, and age-appropriate
- the transcript has passed the Humanizer gate
