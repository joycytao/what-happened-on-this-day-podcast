# Research references

Episode date: 2026-10-07
Chosen subject: Luna 3 photographs the far side of the Moon

## ref-1

NASA Science: First Close-up of the Far Side of Moon (https://science.nasa.gov/resource/first-close-up-of-the-far-side-of-moon/) states that Luna 3 took the first image at 03:30 UT on October 7, 1959 after passing the Moon and looking back at the sunlit far side. The source says Luna 3 took 29 photographs covering about 70 percent of the far side, and that the images were noisy and low resolution but still revealed recognizable features.

Source: NASA Science: First Close-up of the Far Side of Moon
URL: https://science.nasa.gov/resource/first-close-up-of-the-far-side-of-moon/
Source type: official

## ref-2

NASA History: 60 Years Ago, Luna 2 Makes Impact in Moon Race (https://www.nasa.gov/history/60-years-ago-luna-2-makes-impact-in-moon-race/) states that Luna 3 launched on October 4, 1959, passed over the Moon's southern polar region on October 6, and began taking 29 photographs the next day. The source also explains that the spacecraft developed film onboard, scanned it, and transmitted the images to Earth.

Source: NASA History: 60 Years Ago, Luna 2 Makes Impact in Moon Race
URL: https://www.nasa.gov/history/60-years-ago-luna-2-makes-impact-in-moon-race/
Source type: official

## ref-3

NASA Science: First Photo of the Lunar Far Side (https://science.nasa.gov/resource/first-photo-of-the-lunar-far-side/) explains why the discovery mattered: because the Moon always shows Earth the same face, Luna 3 revealed a region that had never been seen before. The source says the far side has fewer large dark maria and is more densely cratered than the familiar near side.

Source: NASA Science: First Photo of the Lunar Far Side
URL: https://science.nasa.gov/resource/first-photo-of-the-lunar-far-side/
Source type: official
