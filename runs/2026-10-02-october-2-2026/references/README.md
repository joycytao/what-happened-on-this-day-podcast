# Research references

Episode date: 2026-10-02
Chosen subject: The first Peanuts comic strip is published

## ref-1

Charles M. Schulz Museum: Timeline Archive (https://schulzmuseum.org/timeline/) states that on October 2, 1950, the first Peanuts comic strip debuted in a four-panel format in seven newspapers nationwide. The same timeline records the earlier 1950 sale to United Feature Syndicate, the pre-publication renaming from Li'l Folks to Peanuts, Schulz's first-month pay, and the January 6, 1952 start of full-color Sunday Peanuts strips.

Source: Charles M. Schulz Museum: Timeline Archive
URL: https://schulzmuseum.org/timeline/
Source type: archive

## ref-2

Charles M. Schulz Museum: Frequently Asked Questions (https://schulzmuseum.org/about-schulz/frequently-asked-questions/) says Peanuts debuted in seven newspapers on October 2, 1950: The Washington Post, The Chicago Tribune, The Minneapolis Star-Tribune, The Allentown Call-Chronicle, The Bethlehem Globe-Times, The Denver Post, and The Seattle Times. The FAQ also states that Charlie Brown appeared in the first strip and first wore his famous zigzag shirt in the December 21, 1950 strip.

Source: Charles M. Schulz Museum: Frequently Asked Questions
URL: https://schulzmuseum.org/about-schulz/frequently-asked-questions/
Source type: reference

## ref-3

Peanuts: Charlie Brown (https://www.peanuts.com/about/charlie-brown) lists Charlie Brown's first appearance as October 2, 1950. The official character page describes him as loyal, determined, responsible for Snoopy, and persistent even when he does not win, which supports an episode angle about children recognizing everyday feelings through a comic character.

Source: Peanuts: Charlie Brown
URL: https://www.peanuts.com/about/charlie-brown
Source type: official

## ref-4

Scholastic Art: Charles M. Schulz (https://art.scholastic.com/pages/topics/art-on-demand/artists/charles-m-schulz.html) says the first Peanuts comic strip appeared in newspapers on October 2, 1950. The article explains Schulz's childhood interest in drawing, comic strips, and his dog Spike, and describes Charlie Brown, Snoopy, Linus, Lucy, Schroeder, and Pigpen for student readers.

Source: Scholastic Art: Charles M. Schulz
URL: https://art.scholastic.com/pages/topics/art-on-demand/artists/charles-m-schulz.html
Source type: reference
